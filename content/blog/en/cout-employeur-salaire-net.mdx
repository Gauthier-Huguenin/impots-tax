# Employer cost vs net salary: France's €100 fiscal journey

The French payslip is a machine that converts a visible employer cost into residual purchasing power for the employee. In this site's educational estimate, reaching about €108 of real purchasing power requires the company to spend roughly €230.

## The full path

| Step | Approximate amount | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Total employer cost | €230 | What the company spends |
| Employer contributions | -€54 | URSSAF, unemployment, supplementary pension |
| Gross salary | €176 | The amount shown on the payslip |
| Employee contributions | -€26 | CSG, CRDS, pension, old age |
| Net before income tax | €150 | Before withholding tax |
| Income tax | -€20 | 30% marginal rate assumption, effective rate around 13% |
| Net after income tax | €130 | Money available |
| VAT on spending | -€22 | Average 20% consumption tax |
| Real purchasing power | €108 | What the €230 really buys |

The dashboard's normalized version starts from a €100 employer base and ends at €48 of real purchasing power. Both readings tell the same story: between the company and the shopping basket, the State shows up repeatedly.

## Why gross salary is not the full story

Gross salary is a useful administrative convention, not the real cost of labor. Employer contributions exist before the gross amount even appears on the payslip. In the site's reference data, they represent about 32% of gross salary.

On the employee side, major contributions include CSG at 9.2% on 98.25% of gross pay, CRDS at 0.5%, capped old-age pension at 6.90%, uncapped old-age pension at 0.40% and AGIRC-ARRCO tier 1 at 3.15%.

## The final layer: VAT

Net salary after income tax is still not tax-free purchasing power. When it is spent, VAT returns. The standard rate is 20%, with reduced rates at 10%, 5.5% and 2.1% depending on the product.

In the €100 net journey estimate, VAT takes back about €22 on spending. The money survived contributions and income tax. It still had to survive checkout.
