# France Flat Tax 2026: why the PFU rises to 31.4%

The Prélèvement Forfaitaire Unique was marketed as simple: 30%, full stop. In 2026, the ordinary rate for covered financial income rises to 31.4%. The income tax portion stays at 12.8%, but social levies increase to 18.6%.

## The new calculation

| Component | 2026 rate |
|---|---:|
| Flat income tax | 12.8% |
| Social levies | 18.6% |
| Total PFU | 31.4% |

The increase comes from CSG on financial capital income, which rises from 9.2% to 10.6%. Total social levies therefore move from 17.2% to 18.6%. The previous 30% total still matters for some products and for income already withheld when paid in 2025.

## What it covers

The PFU applies to dividends, interest, capital gains on securities such as stocks, bonds or funds, and crypto gains upon conversion to euros, with a €305 annual disposal exemption threshold.

For taxable brokerage accounts, this is the central mechanism. The [capital gains outside PEA module](/en/capital-gains) compares it with the PEA, where social levies apply after five years but the 12.8% flat income tax does not.

## Exceptions

Some products retain 17.2% social levies: life insurance and capitalization contracts with surrender value, CEL opened through December 31, 2017, PEL opened through December 31, 2017 during their first twelve years, and exempt PEP products.

Rente-survie and epargne handicap contracts are at 18.6%.

## The progressive-scale option

It remains possible to choose the progressive income tax schedule through box 2OP for investment income and securities capital gains. The option is global. As of 2026, impots.gouv.fr says its irrevocable character has been removed.

This can be attractive if your marginal income tax rate is 11% or lower. For dividends, the 40% allowance only exists under the progressive schedule. For crypto-assets, the progressive-scale option is made through box 3CN and remains independent from box 2OP.
