# France income tax brackets 2026: the 5-step schedule

France's 2026 income tax schedule applies to 2025 income. It has five brackets per tax share, from 0% to 45%. The system looks simple. That is usually the point where French tax administration introduces the family quotient, caps and a differential high-income contribution.

## Quick answer

| Taxable income per share | Rate |
|---|---:|
| Up to €11,600 | 0% |
| €11,601 to €29,579 | 11% |
| €29,580 to €84,577 | 30% |
| €84,578 to €181,917 | 41% |
| Above €181,917 | 45% |

These thresholds apply per tax share. Household income is divided by the number of shares, the bracket schedule is applied, then the resulting tax is multiplied by the number of shares. Straightforward, if your definition of straightforward includes administrative matrix algebra.

## The family quotient changes the reading

One adult counts as 1 share. The first two children add 0.5 share each, and each child from the third onward adds 1 full share. The mechanism lowers the tax burden for families with children, but the benefit is capped at €1,807 per half-share in 2026.

That cap matters. France acknowledges that children cost money, but only within fiscally acceptable boundaries.

## New for 2026: CDHR

The Differential Contribution on High Incomes guarantees a minimum 20% effective income tax rate for the wealthiest households. In plain terms: if deductions, tax niches or capital income bring the effective rate below the target, the State adds a top-up.

The progressive schedule remains the public facade, but high earners also need to watch the overall effective rate.

## Common traps

The marginal rate is not the average rate. If you enter the 30% bracket, only the income inside that bracket is taxed at 30%. The previous brackets remain taxed at 0% and 11%.

The 45% bracket starts above €181,917 per tax share. That means the threshold is not read the same way for a single person and a couple with two shares.

Income tax is only one layer of extraction. To understand the full cost, you also need [social contributions](/en/salary-contributions), then [VAT](/en/vat) when net income is spent.
